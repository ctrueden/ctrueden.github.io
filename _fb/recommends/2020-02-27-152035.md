This review is a cautionary tale for anyone considering doing business with Child & Family Psychological Services.

In summer 2018, my wife and I employed Child & Family Psychological Services to do an academic and psychological assessment of our two children, ages 5 and 7, both of whom had been struggling with behavioral issues and recently diagnosed with ADHD. Over a course of several sessions, Nira Scherz-Busch and her colleague Celia L. Huerta worked with each child individually, administering multiple tests including an IQ test, the Woodcock-Johnson IV Tests of Cognitive Abilities with Achievement Form B and Extended, and a psychological assessment. They also provided consultations with us as parents to understand the test results and troubleshoot our more difficult scenarios as a family.

Nira and Celia were professional and knowledgeable, and their analysis was thorough and helpful. Nira recommended the children attend a school for the gifted & talented, and the test results were used as part of the applications for admission there. Nira also participated in a couple of key meetings with school personnel on school grounds to share expertise and recommendations for dealing with each child’s special needs.

All of those services cost us more than $5000 in total—dozens of hours billed at $140/hour. We were happy to pay, knowing that these test results and reports would be invaluable for the kids throughout their young lives for pursuing needed services, accommodations, diagnoses, etc., as well as for general understanding of our children.

In November 2018, after all assessments had been completed, Nira offered to provide us with either: A) a full report billed at $980 (~7 hours work) per child; B) a brief report at $700 (~5 hours work) per child; or C) a brief letter summarizing each assessment at $540 (~3 hours work) per child. For us, Nira advised that the briefer reports would be sufficient, describing our kids’ developmental and environmental history, explaining test results, and providing diagnoses and recommendations—in her words: “necessary for the kids to get assistance for their ADHD & accelerated G/T services.” We opted for option C.

Unfortunately, now more than a year later, we have still not received these “brief letters”—only a stream of excuses whenever we check in:

Jan 2019: “I am working on the report as we speak.”
April 2019: “We have had numerous problems ... I have been sick. My computer was sick. Working now - but still needs more work. Will be doing both reports soon & email you the background from our transcriptionist.”
May 2019: “Definitely working on it, despite work stress and family emergencies!”
Jun 2019: “I am working on all reports and will likely finish both reports in a week or so!”
Oct 2019: “Reports are in progress - will be done soon!”
early Nov 2019: “All but done & being typed. Background history will be emailed to you by our transcriptionist, for review/edits.”
mid Nov 2019: “Working on it as we speak.”
Dec 2019: “Working on it! I have been sick for the past 3 weeks! Also - it may be a good idea for me to do a SHORT (one session) for an update. Will you be able to bring him sometime soon?” (We declined.)
mid Jan 2020: “Sorry!!!!! As I have told you, I will be working on both this week. We have had a crazy time both at the Clinic and at home. I have been sick on & off for a month! Our cat has been sick & dying ... Will send background to you later this week for your review, as we discussed.  It will come by email from our transcriptionist!”
late Jan 2020: “Re-started working on it last night! I have been sick! Probably will finish this weekend.”

During a phone call in January, I was told that the clinic has a backlog of many dozens of reports still to write.

My family is sympathetic to health issues and other difficult personal circumstances. We walked the road of patience and understanding for many months. We still believe Nira honestly wants to help families with neurodivergent children. But ultimately, regardless of the reasons, Child & Family Psychological Services took our money and then failed to deliver on their commitments in a timely manner. I hope this review helps you to avoid making the same mistakes we did.
