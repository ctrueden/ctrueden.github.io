We are going to interview some students tomorrow for a position in my lab, so I was reading about brainteasers in preparation (and also because they are fun). The [switches puzzle](http://techinterview.org/Puzzles/twoswitches.html) is really cool. Reading it, I got pretty discouraged, but I really like the answer. I definitely started off with a lot of unnecessary assumptions. You can read [the answer](http://w-uh.com/articles/030530-the_two_switches.html), but I suggest you give it a try first. Also, note that the answer is suboptimal—it can be done more efficiently. Anybody know how?  
  
However, the [bad king](http://techinterview.org/Puzzles/fog0000000070.html) problem can go fuck itself. I can't believe how convoluted the answer they give is—totally insane. There is a dirt simple, brain-dead solution (anybody else see it?).  
  
The article through which I discovered most of these puzzles is a [review of "Moving Mount Fuji"](http://w-uh.com/articles/030524-moving_Mount_Fuji.html), which is sometimes interesting and other times ridiculous. For example, I find his solution to the piano tuners question to be horrifyingly complex and error-prone. I would need to have a serious discussion with anyone who came up with that solution to make sure he wasn't a [complexifier](http://www.scottberkun.com/blog/?p=317). :-P  
  
There are also a bunch more such interesting puzzles at [[wu:riddles]](http://www.ocf.berkeley.edu/~wwu/riddles/intro.shtml). And [this page](http://www.thelooneys.com/blogs/jason/archive/2006/04/17/the_microsoft_interview.aspx) is hilarious.
